## Question

A page has a base and the cards and bars on it a surface. In the dark the surface stood lighter
than the base; in the light the order had been laid the other way, a card whiter than a page and a
bar grayer than both, and only the light's cards cast a shadow. Which way does a surface stand
from its base, by how much in each theme, and what tells them apart?

## Options

In the order tried.

1. **The light mirrored from the dark**, a surface darker than its base in the light. Read as a
   page pressed in, the opposite of what a card is.
2. **Higher is lighter in both themes**: the bar and the sidebar on the surface, the page under
   them. The light's base stood 5 levels under white, as the palette had it, and read as a smaller
   step than the dark's 10.
3. **The light's step the dark's over 1.4**, the eye's ratio on grays: 10 levels over 1.4 is about
   7, so the light's base is `oklch(0.979 0 0)`.
4. **A shadow under the light's cards**, kept from the palette, then shown in the dark as well,
   then **in neither**: the step already stands a card off its page, and a shadow in one theme
   alone made the two read differently.
5. **The account's area at the sidebar's foot filled as a field** was tried and taken back: a
   region is a surface, and only what is set into it stands on the base.

## Decision

Options 2, 3 and 4's last.

- **The dark's base is black and its surface 10 levels above it; the light's surface is white and
  its base 7 levels under**, the step the dark's over 1.4.
- **Regions take the surface** -- a card, the shell's top bar, its sidebar -- **and what is set
  into a region stands on the base again**: a search field, a framed button.
- **No shadow in either theme.** A surface stands off its base by its ground and its border alone.
